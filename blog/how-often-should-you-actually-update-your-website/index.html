How Often Should You Actually Update Your Website?

Website Maintenance • Published 16 September 2026

Contents

  • What Actually Counts as "Outdated"?
  • Why Does This Matter So Much to Visitors?
  • What Does Regular Maintenance Actually Involve?

Most business owners treat their website like a photo in a frame, put it up once and leave it exactly as it is. The trouble is a business changes constantly, new prices, new services, new opening hours, new work to show off, and a website that isn't kept in step with that starts quietly working against you instead of for you.

DreamHost's 2026 Local Business Trust Index, a survey of over 1,200 US consumers, found that outdated or inaccurate information is the single biggest frustration people have with business websites, cited by 34% of respondents, ahead of poor design or slow pages. 60% said an outdated-looking site directly hurts how trustworthy a business seems.

What Actually Counts as "Outdated"?

Rarely anything dramatic. It's usually smaller things that build up over time.

  • Old opening hours or contact details, especially after a house move, a new phone number, or seasonal hours nobody updated back.
  • Services or prices that have quietly moved on, so the site is describing last year's business rather than today's.
  • A portfolio or testimonials page frozen in time, still showing the same three projects from launch day.
  • Expired offers or old dates left sitting on the homepage, which is one of the quickest ways to make a business look unattended.
  • Broken links or a form that's stopped sending emails, invisible to the business until someone mentions they tried to get in touch and never heard back.

None of these break the site. They just make it a slightly less accurate, slightly less trustworthy version of the business behind it.

Why Does This Matter So Much to Visitors?

Because a website is how people check a business is legitimate before they commit to it. The same DreamHost survey found 67% of consumers say clear, accurate contact information is the single biggest trust builder on a website, more than design polish or flashy features. People aren't looking for perfection, they're looking for evidence that the business is real, active and actually there.

When that evidence looks stale, the natural assumption isn't "they're just busy," it's "is this place even still open?" That's a hard thing to recover from if it's the first impression.

What Does Regular Maintenance Actually Involve?

Nothing complicated, just a habit of checking a few things on a schedule rather than waiting for a customer to point it out.

  • Reviewing prices, services and opening hours for accuracy every few months.
  • Testing contact forms and phone/email links to make sure enquiries actually arrive.
  • Refreshing photos and recent work so the site reflects what the business looks like now.
  • Checking for broken links, especially after any changes elsewhere on the site.
  • Keeping the software behind the site up to date, which is worth doing regardless of whether that's WordPress, a drag and drop builder, or something custom-coded.

A site that's checked and lightly tidied every quarter rarely needs a big overhaul. One that's left for two or three years usually does, and by then it's not just outdated information, it's often slower too, which ties into why website speed actually matters in its own right.

For a Sheffield business where the website is often the first proper look a potential customer gets, keeping it current is a small, ongoing job rather than a big occasional one.
