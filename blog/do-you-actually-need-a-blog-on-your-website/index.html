Do You Actually Need a Blog on Your Website?

Published 22 July 2026

Contents

  • What Does a Blog Actually Do for a Website?
  • Does Blogging Actually Help With SEO?
  • What if You Don't Have Time to Write Regularly?
  • What Should a Small Business Actually Blog About?

  • Frequently Asked Questions

Short answer: for most local businesses, yes, but not in the way people usually imagine it.

A lot of business owners picture blogging as writing for the sake of it, filling a page nobody asked for. That's not really what it's for. A blog is one of the few parts of a website you can keep adding to indefinitely, and each new page is another chance to answer a question a potential customer is actually searching for.

What Does a Blog Actually Do for a Website?

It gives you somewhere to answer the specific questions your customers already ask you. Every business fields the same handful of questions on the phone or in person, how much does this cost, how long does it take, what's included, is this the right option for my situation. A blog turns those answers into pages that can be found by someone searching before they've ever spoken to you.

It also gives a website more to be found for. A site with five pages can only ever rank for five pages' worth of searches. A site that's steadily adding content has far more surface area for someone to land on.

Does Blogging Actually Help With SEO?

Yes, and it's one of the more measurable parts of a marketing budget.

Orbit Media's long-running annual blogging survey, one of the largest ongoing studies of its kind, found that bloggers who spend six or more hours on a single post are twice as likely to report strong results as those who rush it. Effort per page matters more than how many pages you publish. That's good news for a small business that can't realistically post every week, one properly thought-through post genuinely outperforms several rushed ones.

What if You Don't Have Time to Write Regularly?

This is the most common reason blogs get abandoned, not a lack of belief in the idea, just a lack of time to keep up with it.

The fix usually isn't posting more often. It's picking a realistic pace and sticking to it. One well-considered post a month, actually answering a real customer question, outperforms four rushed ones that say very little. Consistency matters more than volume.

What Should a Small Business Actually Blog About?

The questions you already get asked. That's almost always the best starting point, not keyword research, not trends, just the things customers ask you directly.

A few reliable categories: pricing and process questions, comparisons between options you offer, common mistakes customers make before they come to you, and updates about your own work that double as proof you're active and doing well. It's also worth thinking about how this differs from posting on social media, which we've covered in whether social media can replace a website. A social post disappears from feeds within a day. A blog post on your own site keeps working, and keeps showing up in search, for years.

What This Means for Your Business

For a business in Sheffield competing with others offering a similar service, a blog is one of the few genuinely low-cost ways to widen how many searches you can be found for, without paying for ads. It won't fix a website that's fundamentally broken though. If it's been a while since anything on your site changed, it's worth checking against the signs your website needs updating first. But on top of a website that already works, a blog is one of the highest-leverage additions you can make.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should a small business actually post a blog?
Once a month, done properly, is a realistic and effective pace for most small businesses. Consistency matters far more than frequency.

Does a blog only help SEO, or does it help customers directly too?
Both. It helps a site get found in search, and it gives potential customers real answers before they ever pick up the phone, which tends to make the eventual conversation shorter and warmer.

What if I don't know what to write about?
Start with the questions you already answer out loud every week. If a customer has asked it more than once, it's worth a page.
