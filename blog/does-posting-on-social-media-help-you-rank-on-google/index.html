Does Posting on Social Media Help You Rank on Google?

Does Posting on Social Media Help You Rank on Google?

A client asked us this recently after noticing a competitor posting on Instagram almost daily while ranking above them on Google. It's a reasonable thing to wonder. It's also not really how it works.

What Google Has Actually Said About This

Directly, no. Google has addressed this specifically, more than once. According to reporting from Search Engine Journal, Google's John Mueller has repeatedly confirmed that likes, shares and follower counts are not used in Google's ranking algorithm. Most links posted on social platforms are nofollowed anyway, which means they carry no direct SEO weight even as links.

Google's former head of web spam, Matt Cutts, gave the practical reason for this back in 2014: social profiles can be created and manipulated too easily, and Google can't reliably treat that activity as a trustworthy signal in real time.

Then Why Do Active Social Accounts Seem to Rank Better?

Correlation, not causation, and it's a common trap. A business that posts consistently on social media is often also the kind of business doing other things right: publishing content people actually link to, getting mentioned by other sites, and building enough of a name that people search for the business directly by name.

Those downstream effects, more backlinks, more brand searches, more direct traffic, are genuine ranking signals. The social posting itself isn't. It's just often sitting next to the things that are.

What Social Media Is Actually Good For

It's not that social media does nothing for a business. It just does a different job than SEO does.

  • Staying visible to people who already know you, without needing them to search for anything.
  • Driving referral traffic directly to your website, which is separate from organic search traffic entirely.
  • Building the kind of brand recognition that eventually turns into someone typing your business name into Google.
  • Humanising a business in a way a website alone often can't.

All useful. None of it a shortcut around actual SEO work.

Where This Leaves a Small Business

If a Sheffield business is choosing between spending an hour on social media or an hour improving the website itself, the website usually wins for search visibility specifically. Social media builds an audience you already have to reach yourself. Search brings in people who didn't know you existed yet.

That only works, though, if the website people eventually land on actually does its job. We've written before about why a website isn't converting even when it gets traffic, which is worth checking if social is sending visitors your way but enquiries aren't following. It's also worth knowing what a genuinely strong site looks like in the first place, covered in what makes a good business website.

Run both well and they support each other. Just don't expect the posting schedule to move the needle on Google by itself, because it won't.
